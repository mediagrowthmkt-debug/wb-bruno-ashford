The AI Slowdown Drama

Anthropic's CEO says: slow down

We Must Pace the Frontier: I’ve written a new essay on why the AI industry should slow down, with a three-part plan for doing so. Anthropic is unilaterally committing to the first of these steps. We’ll provide third-party evaluators with permanent, employee-level access to our

— @DarioAmodei https://twitter.com/DarioAmodei/status/2098773920774074715

Dario Amodei, who runs Anthropic (the lab behind Claude), published an essay arguing the whole AI industry should slow its pace, and committed his company to permanent independent evaluators watching its models. In plain terms: safety is turning into a business and regulatory lever, not just an ethics talking point. That shapes which AI tools will be allowed, and who gets to sell them.

The sharper read

Read the timing, not just the words. The letter dropped, and the biggest names lined up behind it within hours. When the largest labs agree the frontier should slow down, the safe bet is that the rules that follow will quietly favor the companies already at the top. For you, that means less choice and higher prices later if you wait.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@DarioAmodei)Full essay: We Must Pace the Frontier

OpenAI agrees within hours

I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier. This has been a primary topic of discussions we've had at OpenAI in recent weeks. Committing to having independent evaluators with employee-like access is a great idea, and we will do the same. We'll have more to share soon.

— @sama https://twitter.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910

Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI (the maker of ChatGPT), publicly agreed with Dario almost immediately and promised the same independent oversight. When the two biggest players in AI align on the same message this fast, it is worth paying attention to what comes next.

The sharper read

The real risk for a business owner is not the safety pledge. It is regulation heavy enough to lock small players and cheaper open models out, so you are nudged toward buying the incumbents' approved products down the road. The other side of the debate argues trust and alignment are becoming the very features that separate the models, which is a healthier framing for competition.

Jev, the AI That Only Decides

A ChatGPT co-inventor ships 'Jev'

After co-inventing ChatGPT, I kept asking myself: why have superhuman chat models not led to AGI? I’ve spent the last 2 years in stealth building a new way to train models (RLCD), and a new type of frontier AI model that we are releasing today: Jev • 20-200x faster • 40-400x

— @CompleteSkeptic https://twitter.com/CompleteSkeptic/status/2099925682726002904

One of the people who helped invent ChatGPT spent two years quietly building something different, and just released it. It is called Jev, and instead of writing paragraphs it makes decisions. The pitch: 20 to 200 times faster and 40 to 400 times cheaper than a normal AI. That is a brand-new category of tool, not a slightly better chatbot.

The sharper read

This is the story of the week for anyone who runs software with lots of if-this-then-that logic. A model that only decides, cheaply and fast, is exactly what most business automation actually needs. The first thing worth testing is your own repetitive triage: which lead is hot, which ticket is urgent, which invoice looks off.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@CompleteSkeptic)Technical post: Typesafe / System One models

The proof: 384 news items in 25s for $0.19

Jev is INSANE. 🤯 in 24.9 seconds it read 384 news from this morning and told 15 brands which stories to hop onto today, for $0.19. Claude Opus 5, running on the same feed at the same time, got through 4/384 and cost $0.77. per headline that is ~390x cheaper, and the answer

— @elvissun https://twitter.com/elvissun/status/2100951347080421409

Here is Jev in a real test, not a demo. It read 384 news items in about 25 seconds and sorted which stories 15 brands should jump on, for 19 cents. A top-tier chatbot running the same feed at the same time got through 4 of the 384 and cost 77 cents. Roughly 390 times cheaper per decision.

The sharper read

Numbers like these change what is worth automating. Tasks you never bothered to automate because the AI cost too much per run suddenly become almost free. Automated triage of anything (news, leads, support, orders) is the obvious first place to look.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@elvissun)

LLMs generate answers. Jev decides.

this is the easiest way to understand Jev: LLMs generate answers. Jev makes decisions. that sounds like a small difference, but it actually changes the entire use case. say you give a normal LLM this: “here’s a user, their account history, payment behavior, support chats,

— @paarangatrai https://twitter.com/paarangatrai/status/2100113737097367896

The clearest explanation going around: give both the same customer information, and a normal AI writes a paragraph, while Jev returns a structured decision, like risk high or low, with a confidence percentage. That difference sounds small, but for any system that has to choose rather than explain, it is huge.

The sharper read

If your business runs on rules (approve or flag, prioritize or park, escalate or hold), a decide-only model slots straight in, at a fraction of the cost and with far less of the making-things-up that plagues chatbots. It does not replace ChatGPT or Claude. It sits next to them where a fast, cheap yes-or-no is what you actually need.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@paarangatrai)

A major platform is switching its default

We're seeing extraordinary results from @typesafeai. Default mode in 𝚏𝚡 is auto, with a safety reviewer analyzing every command. That reviewer runs on GPT Luna today. Jev is up to 18x faster (p95) *and* more accurate. It's coming to @vercel AI Gateway and likely new default.

— @rauchg https://twitter.com/rauchg/status/2100307962262872105

Guillermo Rauch runs Vercel, a platform millions of apps are built on. He says their automated safety reviewer runs on one AI today, but Jev is far faster and more accurate, and is likely to become the new default. Translation: decision-models are already moving into real production systems, not just experiments.

The sharper read

This matters because it is a production test at scale, not a hyped screenshot. When infrastructure this widely used quietly swaps its default, it is a strong signal that the tool is ready for real work. Worth tracking as it becomes the standard the tools you use are built on.

Claude Becomes an Office Suite

Chat and 'do it for me' become one Claude

Claude Cowork and chat are merging into one Claude. Ask a quick question or hand over a report, and Claude takes it from there, even after you close your laptop. If something's unclear, Claude asks—you keep the final say. Rolling out to Pro and Max over the next few weeks.

— @claudeai https://twitter.com/claudeai/status/2100258490740539730

Anthropic is merging two things into a single Claude: the quick-answer chat you know, and the mode where you hand it a whole job and it keeps working, even after you close the laptop. So you can ask a fast question or delegate a full report from the same place. The assistant starts to behave like a coworker.

The sharper read

This is the direction that matters for owners. The value is not a smarter chat, it is an assistant that finishes work on its own and only comes back to you when it needs a decision. Rolling out over the coming weeks to paid plans.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@claudeai)Official: Cowork is now Claude

Claude now makes docs and slides

Claude Code can now make docs and slides as artifacts, so you can share and collaborate with other people on your team. For example, ask Claude to make a spec as a doc, share it to your teammates for feedback, and when it's ready ask Claude to implement.

— @trq212 https://twitter.com/trq212/status/2100270818433643000

Claude can now create shareable documents and slides you can collaborate on with your team, right inside the tool. Quietly, that puts Anthropic into the same space as Google Docs and Microsoft Office, from the AI angle.

The sharper read

For a small team this is real leverage: draft a spec or a deck, share it, get feedback, and have the AI implement the changes, without bouncing between five apps. The office suite is slowly becoming something you talk to rather than something you fill in by hand.

The Context That Rots

'Someone may have solved context decay'

too be honest not enough hype around this Cursor may have genuinely solved context decay. if they did. this really was the last major puzzle piece for a single engineer to build enterprise level software.

— @corbin_braun https://twitter.com/corbin_braun/status/2098496865931305400

A widely shared claim: one of the popular AI coding tools may have cracked context rot, the problem where an AI slowly forgets or muddles what you told it earlier in a long task. If true, it removes the last big blocker stopping a single person from building serious, company-grade software.

The sharper read

Memory is the new battleground. Whether or not this specific claim holds, the winners of the next stretch will be the tools that keep long, complex work coherent from start to finish. For a lean business, better memory means one person plus AI can ship what used to take a team.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@corbin_braun)

Days later, Claude ships the same idea

Projects now run from one conversation, starting in Claude Code. You describe what needs doing, and Claude directs parallel threads that keep working after you close your laptop. In beta today for select Pro and Max users in cloud sessions; coming to all Claude users soon.

— @claudeai https://twitter.com/claudeai/status/2100632677904744716

Right after, Anthropic answered with Projects: one conversation that directs several tasks running in parallel, and keeps working after you close the laptop. It starts in the cloud for select plans and is coming to everyone soon.

The sharper read

When rival tools ship the same idea within days of each other, that idea has become the standard, not a gamble. The honest caveat: cloud-only at first, with no direct access to your local files yet, so it is worth waiting for the full version before you build a workflow around it.

What Changes in Your Automation

One standard for AI instructions is winning

We're adding support for AGENTS.md to Claude Code. Starting today in version 2.1.277, if there is no CLAUDE.md in a folder, Claude will check for and use AGENTS.md. You can toggle this behavior in /config.

— @trq212 https://twitter.com/trq212/status/2101009392611278961

After pushback from the developer community, Claude Code now also reads a file called AGENTS.md, the same instruction file other AI tools already use. It sounds technical, but the takeaway is simple: the industry is converging on one shared way to tell AI agents how your business works, which makes your automations more portable and less locked to a single vendor.

The sharper read

This is quietly good news if you are building on AI. A common standard means the rules and context you write for one tool carry over to the next, so you are not rebuilding from scratch every time a better model appears. Less lock-in, more freedom to switch.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@trq212)Official change in the repository

OpenAI explains why a tool got worse

Hi Astra users. A reset and a quick update on quality issues that have been posted around. Working with some of you, we have found and fixed the following issues: - Some skills written for previous models were triggering too often or preventing the model from checking its work.

— @thsottiaux https://twitter.com/thsottiaux/status/2098612714704891959

OpenAI publicly admitted that one of its coding tools had dropped in quality, including add-ons that were firing too often and getting in the model's way, and shipped fixes. That kind of transparency, after weeks of user complaints, is worth noting.

The sharper read

The practical lesson for any owner relying on these tools: quality is not fixed, it moves as vendors tune their systems. Keep a simple check on the work your AI produces so a silent dip does not cost you before you notice it.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@thsottiaux)

'I was wrong': a builder changes his mind

I was not expecting things to go this way, but I think MCPs are better than CLIs for most integrations. The models have gotten much better at tool calling, we can defer tools &amp; MCP is now stateless. If you need to compose/filter data, add params like query to your MCP tools.

— @trq212 https://twitter.com/trq212/status/2099958388230873165

An engineer who helped build Claude Code publicly reversed his opinion: he now thinks a certain way of plugging tools into AI (called MCP) beats the older method for most jobs, because models handle tools better now and the approach is cleaner. Insider debates like this preview which building blocks your future automations will rest on.

The sharper read

You do not need the jargon. The signal is that the plumbing under AI automation is settling toward a simpler, more reliable standard, which lowers the cost and risk of connecting the tools you already pay for. When the people who built the tools change their minds in public, the ground is shifting.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@trq212)

Who Wants Out of the Cloud

A top law firm buys its own AI servers

this is Dario and sama's worst nightmare - A law firm buying Nvidia servers. Latham &amp; Watkins is building an in-house AI stack.. this is US’s second-largest law firm with $8.3 Billion in revenue last year And now it has - - Nvidia hardware it controls - open-weight models

— @ayushtweetshere https://twitter.com/ayushtweetshere/status/2099380823229403308

One of the largest US law firms, with 8.3 billion dollars in yearly revenue, is building its own in-house AI: hardware it owns plus open models it controls, instead of renting everything from the big AI labs. Big companies increasingly want AI on their own terms, off someone else's meter.

The sharper read

This is the counter-trend to watch. As using AI at scale gets expensive, more large firms will pull it in-house to control cost and data. For a smaller business the lesson is the same in miniature: know what you are renting, what it costs as you grow, and where owning your own setup pays off.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@ayushtweetshere)

Two Closing Reflections

Cheap to build, not to know what to build

AI has collapsed the cost of building products, but not the cost of knowing what to build. - The old loop: spec, scope, build - The new loop: build, play, design, ship Prototypes are cheap. Great products still require taste, judgment, and a clear understanding of what users

— @a16z https://twitter.com/a16z/status/2099501195006238899

A leading investment firm made the point of the week: AI has collapsed the cost of building products, but not the cost of knowing what to build. The old way was spec, then build. The new way is build, play, design, ship. Taste and judgment are now the real moat.

The sharper read

This is the whole game for a founder in 2026. When anyone can build fast, the advantage moves to whoever knows what is worth building and for whom. That part is human, and it is exactly the work AI cannot do for you.

ReferencesOriginal post on X (@a16z)Full article: product management

The end of flow state

The end of flow state You wake up, excited for the day to start. You have a feature idea you are looking forward to implement. You make your coffee. Sit in front of the laptop. Put on that techno set you love. The house is empty for the next 8 hours. No one can interrupt you.

— @dannypostma https://twitter.com/dannypostma/status/2098751613825650896

A widely shared, honest reflection: for people who used to build things by hand, doing the work felt like magic, and watching the AI generate it can feel hollow. It is the human, emotional side of this shift, and it is worth sitting with rather than dismissing.

The sharper read

There are two ways people are landing. Some find a new kind of flow in directing many AI agents at once. Others just feel bored while the machine works. Which one you become is a choice about how you set up your days, and it decides whether AI gives you your time back or quietly takes the part of the work you loved.
