How to Build AI Agents on Your Computer (No Code)

How to build AI agents on your computer without writing code. This is the full walkthrough behind the video: install the tools, give AI access to your machine, build a memory, create skills, assemble a team of agents, and connect your CRM and calendar so agents do the real work in your business. You talk, they build.

Bruno Ashford September 09, 2026 · 9 min read

Most people using AI are stuck typing questions into a chat and copying the answers back into their work by hand. There is a far more powerful way, and once you see it you cannot unsee it: you build AI agents directly on your computer, give them access to your files and tools, and let them do the actual work. No degree in programming required. In the video above I set the whole thing up live, and this guide is the written, step-by-step version so you can follow along and build the same engine inside your own business.

The promise is simple. By the end of this, you will understand exactly how to build AI agents on your computer with no code, how they differ from a chatbot, and how to wire them into the tools you already run your business on. You talk, they build.

Chatbot vs. agent: the shift that changes everything

There are two ways to use AI, and the gap between them decides who pulls ahead over the next two years.

The first is the chat. You open ChatGPT or Claude, ask a question, read the reply, and then do the real work yourself: format the document, save it, send it to the right person, follow up. The AI answered. You are still the worker.

The second is the agent. You give AI access to your computer. Because everything on a machine is ultimately code, an AI that can run code can do everything you do on that computer: organize files, build spreadsheets, use any program, produce documents, and deliver them to email, WhatsApp, SMS, wherever you want. You ask once, and the work comes back done, filed, and sent.

That is the entire difference in one line: the chat answers you, the agent does the work. This guide is about the second way, and it is exactly what almost nobody outside AI-native companies is doing yet.

What you need before you start

You do not need a developer, a big budget, or a computer science background. You need three things.

  • - A code editor. I use VS Code. It is free, and its real value here is that it gives you a clean home for your work: one folder per client, and inside each folder everything you produce for them, from carousels and design to finance documents, integrations, and marketing strategies. It is your filing cabinet and your workshop in one place.
  • - The coding layer of AI. This is the AI that runs on your machine and executes tasks, not just chats. I use Claude Code. You can run it from your terminal or from inside VS Code. This is the piece that turns "answer my question" into "do the job."
  • - A plan for what to automate first. Before you build anything, pick one repetitive task you or your staff do on a computer every week. That is your first agent. Resist the urge to automate everything at once.

With those in place, you can build your first working agent the same day.

Step 1: Install the tools

Start by downloading and installing VS Code, then install Claude Code so it runs inside it. You can also run Claude Code straight from your terminal if you prefer; both work. I use the higher-usage plan because I run a lot through it every day, and heavy use consumes tokens, so pick the tier that matches how much you will actually lean on it.

The mental model to hold on to: from this moment, the work a person would otherwise do by hand on the computer can be done by the AI on your computer instead. Create a folder, build a website, draft a document, pull a report. You ask, it executes.

Step 2: Create your working folder and the memory

Open VS Code and create a folder for your business. This is where everything lives. Inside it, you will keep a folder for each client and, at the top level, the most important files in the whole system: the memory.

The memory is where the AI learns who you are. In practice it is a file, usually named CLAUDE.md, that acts as the brain of the operation, plus a MEMORY.md that accumulates what the system learns over time. In it you write your business context, your brand, your rules, how you like things done, and what should never happen. This is what lets an agent produce a document that already knows your colors, your logo, and your voice, without you re-explaining it every time.

Think of the memory as onboarding. You would not expect a new hire to do great work on day one with no context. The memory is the context, written once and reused forever. The better you write it, the better every agent performs.

Step 3: Create your first skill

A skill is a single, repeatable procedure the AI follows the same way every time. "Generate an invoice." "Draft a client contract." "Turn a video into a set of clips." Each of those becomes a skill.

Here is the part most people miss. You can use Claude Code in two ways. You can use it raw, typing "create a website for me" and taking what comes back. Or you can train it: teach it, step by step, how to produce the best version of that output for your business, and save those instructions as a skill. Once a skill exists, the work comes out consistent and high quality every single time, because the how is written down, not improvised.

Start with one skill for the task you picked in your plan. Get it right. Then build the next one. This is how you compound quality instead of chasing it.

Step 4: Build your team of agents

Now the fun part. An agent is a worker with a name, a role, and access to your data. In my own setup, I have a crew, and I call each one by name to do its job.

  • - A design agent that produces logos, email art, carousels, and social posts, already trained on the brand.
  • - A finance agent that handles the money in and money out, and can generate estimates and invoices.
  • - A contracts agent that produces a validated contract for every new client.
  • - A presentations agent that builds decks on demand.
  • - A messaging agent connected to WhatsApp, so client conversations live inside the system.
  • - A traffic agent that schedules and runs ad campaigns.
  • - A video agent and a distribution agent that edit content and publish it across channels.
  • - Even a podcast agent that watches episodes and cuts clips automatically.

To trigger one, you simply call it by name and tell it what you want. "Frank, build these pages." And Frank, the agent I use for websites, builds them. Every document, every page, every asset you saw in the video came out of an agent, not a person clicking around.

I run all of this from my own control platform, which I also built with the coding layer of AI. It is where the agents live, where I can plug in any AI to power them, and where the finance, the WhatsApp conversations, the client groups, and the CRM all sit in one place, with the agents able to reach them. You do not have to start there. You can start with a folder, a memory file, and one agent. The platform is where it goes once it is working.

Step 5: Connect your tools with MCP

An agent is only as useful as what it can reach. This is where MCP comes in. MCP is the layer that lets your agents read and act inside the apps you already run your business on: your CRM, your calendar, your messaging, your analytics.

Connect them once. After that, an agent can pull a contact from the CRM, check your calendar for availability, send a client a message, or read a live report, all on command, all on your real data. Without this connection, an agent is guessing. With it, the agent works. This is the piece that turns a clever assistant into a genuine part of your operation. No MCP, no future.

Agent vs. skill, in one line

People mix these up, so keep it clean.

  • - An agent is the who: a named worker with a role and access to your data.
  • - A skill is the how: a single repeatable procedure that worker follows.

One agent can hold many skills. You build the skills so the work is consistent, and you call the agents to get the jobs done. Get this distinction and the whole system clicks into place.

FAQ

Questions people ask about this

How do I build AI agents on my computer without coding?

You do not write software. You install a coding-layer AI like Claude Code, point it at a working folder on your machine, and then instruct it in plain English. Because everything on a computer is ultimately code, an AI that can run code can create files, build documents, use your programs, and deliver the result wherever you want. You give it a memory file so it learns your business, then create small skills for the tasks you repeat. You are configuring a worker by talking to it, not programming it.

What is the difference between an AI agent and an AI skill?

An agent is a worker with a role, like a design agent, a finance agent, or a sales agent. It has a name, a job, and access to your data, and you call it when you want that job done. A skill is a single repeatable procedure the agent follows, such as "generate an invoice" or "draft a contract." Agents are the who; skills are the how. One agent can hold many skills, and you build skills once so the work comes out the same way every time.

What do I need to get started building AI agents?

Three things. First, a code editor like VS Code so you can keep a clean folder for your work and your clients. Second, Claude Code installed inside it, which is the AI that runs on your machine and executes tasks. Third, a memory file, usually a CLAUDE.md and a MEMORY.md, where you write who your business is, how you work, and your rules. With those in place you can create your first skill and your first agent the same day.

How do AI agents connect to my CRM, calendar, and other tools?

Through MCP, the layer that lets an agent read and act inside the apps you already use. You connect your CRM, your calendar, your messaging, and your analytics once, and after that the agent can pull a contact, check availability, send a message, or read a report on command. Without that connection an agent is stuck guessing; with it, the agent works on your real data. As the saying goes, no MCP, no future.

Can AI agents really run parts of my business, not just chat?

Yes, and that is the whole point of building them on your computer instead of only using a chat window. A chat answers your question and leaves the work to you. An agent with access to your files and tools does the work end to end: it creates the document, issues the invoice, publishes the content, or pulls the report, without you doing the manual steps. You keep the strategy and the client relationships. The agents carry the repetitive execution.
