How to See Competitors Keywords in Google Ads With AI

For business owners and marketing agencies: see every keyword your competitors bid on, what each click costs, and how much they pay, using AI instead of a paid tool. The result is more leads, smarter spend, and a real edge over rivals. Watch it done live.

Bruno Ashford September 07, 2026 · 18 min read

How to see competitors keywords in Google Ads with AI: connect the Google Ads API to an AI assistant, point it at a competitor's website, and ask it to return every keyword their services compete for, the real cost per click, and the monthly search volume. That is the whole method, and in the video above I run it live, in seconds.

This is written for two people. If you own a business, this is how you find out exactly what your competitors are paying to win customers, so you can win the same customers for less and stop guessing where your marketing budget goes. If you run a marketing agency, this is how you walk into a pitch already knowing the client's market cold, and deliver competitive intelligence that used to require an expensive stack of tools. For founders and agency owners alike, the payoff is the same: more leads, smarter spend, and a measurable edge over rivals.

Most owners assume a competitor's Google Ads strategy is a black box. It is not. You can see every keyword they bid on, what each click actually costs, and how much they are paying to show up, without buying a single paid SEO tool. It is not a trick or a scraper. It works because Google Ads, like almost every platform your business already uses, connects to AI through an API. Once that connection exists, you stop marketing on a hunch and start marketing on evidence, which is exactly why learning how to see competitors keywords this way beats renting the answer from a monthly subscription.

What you can actually see

When the AI is wired into Google Ads, a competitor's whole keyword footprint stops being hidden. In one request you can pull, for any business in your niche:

  • - Every keyword their services would compete for, expanded automatically from their website and offer.
  • - The real cost per click for each keyword, so you know what it costs to show up.
  • - The monthly search volume, so you know how many people are actually looking.
  • - The competition level, so you can tell a crowded auction from an open one.

In the video I do exactly this for a client I am about to run ads for. I ask the AI to open the client's website, read every service they provide, and then find the minimum I could pay per click for each of those keywords. What comes back is real data from Google: keywords like "deck builder price" at around $6 a click, "cost of a garage addition," and "finish carpenter," each with its own price and demand attached.

That single answer replaces hours of manual research and a monthly subscription to a competitor-spying tool.

Why the real cost per click changes everything

Knowing the cost per click for every keyword in your niche is not a vanity metric. It is a map of where the money is easy and where it is hard.

Some keywords are expensive because everyone is fighting over them. Others carry real buying intent but almost no competition, which means you can win the click for a fraction of the price. Until you see the numbers side by side, you are bidding blind. Once you see them, you can make a deliberate choice: which services are worth paying to advertise for, and which are cheaper to earn through organic search instead.

This is the question I hear from every founder running paid traffic: "Which of my services costs me the most, and which costs me the least?" With the data pulled, the answer stops being an opinion. Now you know, because the AI is connected to Google and reads it straight from the source.

From one keyword to a full strategy

The real power is not looking up a single keyword. It is turning one pull of data into a complete plan. In the video, once the first list comes back, I keep going:

  1. 1. Expand the winners. I ask the AI to generate more keywords based on the best ones, so a handful of seed terms becomes a full map of the niche, each with its own cost per click.
  2. 2. Rank the opportunities. With volume and cost attached to every keyword, the expensive-and-crowded terms separate cleanly from the cheap-and-winnable ones.
  3. 3. Split paid from organic. The high-cost, high-intent keywords become candidates for SEO, so you rank for the traffic your competitors are paying for. The rest can run as ads where the math works.
  4. 4. Automate the reporting. I can tell the AI to email me every keyword and its price, every day if I want, so the intelligence keeps flowing without anyone lifting a finger.

That last step is the one people underestimate. This does not have to be a one-time exercise you do before a campaign. It can become a standing system that watches your market and reports back on its own, the same way a good AI operating system runs the rest of a company's repetitive work.

Turn the same data into SEO, not just ads

Everyone thinks of competitor keyword data as an ads tool. The bigger prize is organic search. The exact list that tells you what to bid on also tells you what to build content and pages around.

Here is the logic: a keyword with high cost per click and steady search volume is a keyword your competitors are literally paying money to appear for. If you can rank for it organically, you earn that same traffic for free, forever, instead of renting it click by click. So the smartest play is to take the expensive keywords from your pull and turn them into pages, guides, and service content designed to rank.

That is why competitor keyword research feeds both sides of your marketing. One request, two engines: paid traffic where speed matters, and SEO where compounding matters. This is the same connected thinking behind sound marketing strategy, where the ad, the keyword, and the landing page are treated as one system instead of three separate jobs.

Why AI beats the old competitor tools

Paid competitor-research tools have existed for years. They estimate a rival's keywords from the outside and charge a monthly fee for the guess. AI connected to the Google Ads API is a different thing entirely, for three reasons.

  • - It reads the source, not an estimate. You are querying Google's own data, so the cost-per-click and volume figures are the real ones, not a third party's approximation.
  • - It does exactly what you ask. Instead of clicking through a dashboard someone else designed, you describe what you want in plain English: expand these keywords, rank them by cost, email me the top ten daily. The tool bends to your question.
  • - It connects to everything else. The keyword data does not sit in a silo. It can flow into your CRM, your reporting, and your content plan, because the same AI is connected to those too. That is the whole idea of running a business on connected agents rather than a drawer full of disconnected apps.

None of this means the paid tools are useless. It means they are no longer the only door. For most business owners, the AI approach is cheaper, more flexible, and does more, because it works the way you think instead of the way a software company decided.

The step-by-step: how to see competitors keywords with AI

Here is the exact sequence from the video, written out so you can follow it. None of it requires you to write code; it requires the connection to exist and then plain-English requests.

  1. 1. Connect Google Ads to your AI assistant. This is the one-time setup. Once the Google Ads API is wired in, the AI can read the same keyword, cost, and volume data the platform holds.
  2. 2. Point it at a competitor. Give it a competitor's website and tell it to read every service they offer. The AI builds the seed list for you instead of you brainstorming keywords by hand.
  3. 3. Ask for the real cost per click. Request the minimum you could pay per click for each of those service keywords. What comes back is Google's own number, not an estimate.
  4. 4. Pull the volume and competition. Ask it to attach monthly search volume and the competition level to each keyword, so you see demand and difficulty next to the price.
  5. 5. Expand the best terms. Take the strongest keywords and ask the AI to generate variations, so a handful of seeds becomes a full map of the niche.
  6. 6. Sort and decide. Have it rank everything by cost, by volume, or by opportunity, and you have a prioritized plan for both ads and SEO.

That is the entire method. The magic is not any single step; it is that all of them happen in one conversation, against real Google data, in minutes.

How keyword variations multiply the value

One keyword is a data point. Variations are a strategy. When you ask the AI to make variations of your top terms, it does not just add plurals. It surfaces the different ways real people phrase the same intent: "deck builder price," "cost to build a deck," "deck installation cost," each with its own volume and cost per click.

This matters for two reasons. First, some variations are far cheaper to win than the obvious head term, so you can capture the same buyer for less. Second, the variations reveal the long tail, the specific, lower-competition phrases that are the easiest place for a smaller business to rank organically. The head term tells you the market exists. The variations tell you where the open doors are.

The free way: Google Ads Transparency Center

You do not even need the API connection to start. Google runs a free tool called the Ads Transparency Center where you can look up any advertiser and see the live ads they are currently running, across Search, Display, and YouTube. It will not hand you a full keyword list with prices, but it shows you a competitor's actual messaging and offers.

The powerful move is to combine the two. Use the Transparency Center to see what a rival is advertising, then hand those ads and offers to the AI and ask it to expand them into a full keyword and cost map. The free tool gives you the starting signal; the AI turns that signal into a plan. For a business just getting started, that pairing is a complete competitor-intelligence workflow at almost no cost.

Common mistakes when reading competitor keyword data

Seeing the data is only half the job. Reading it wrong is easy, and it costs money. A few traps to avoid:

  • - Chasing the most expensive keywords. A high cost per click is a signal of demand, not a mandate to bid. Sometimes the smart move is to rank for that term organically and let competitors overpay for the click.
  • - Ignoring the long tail. The obvious head terms are crowded and pricey. The specific, lower-volume variations are where a smaller business actually wins, both in ads and in search.
  • - Treating one pull as the whole picture. Costs and competitors move. A single snapshot ages fast, which is why turning this into a standing report beats a one-time export.
  • - Copying a competitor's list wholesale. Their keywords fit their offer and their margins, not yours. Use their data to find gaps, then choose the terms that match what you actually sell profitably.

The data is only as good as the decision it drives. The goal is not to know what competitors bid on; it is to spend smarter than they do.

The questions people ask most

A few things come up every time I show this to a business owner or an agency owner.

How can I see competitors in Google Ads? Through the free Ads Transparency Center for their live ads, and through an AI assistant connected to the Google Ads API for their full keyword and cost picture. The two together give you both the messaging and the numbers.

How much does Google Ads pay per 1,000 views? That question mixes up the two sides of Google. As an advertiser, you pay per click or per thousand impressions to show ads; the cost varies by keyword, exactly the data this method reveals. Earning money per 1,000 views is the publisher side (AdSense/YouTube), which is a different program. For competitor research, the number that matters is what a click costs you to win, not what a view pays a publisher.

Who is Google Ads' biggest competitor? For advertisers, the main alternatives are Meta Ads, Microsoft Advertising, and increasingly retail and social platforms. But the more useful competitor to study is not another ad network, it is the other businesses bidding against you on the exact keywords you care about, which is precisely what this workflow uncovers.
